### .forbidden by nurture (lifecycle invariants)

| # | the attempt | why forbidden (the harm) |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | a second daemon of one key spawns while one lives | every alert doubles and the mechanic acts twice; github spend doubles. the wish: one per machine per version |
| F2 | a clone registers its watch with a daemon of another key | that daemon's code may misread a watch record it does not know, and alert wrong or not at all. the wish: a version never shares |
| F3 | a daemon outlives its last watch | an orphan holds memory forever; rhachet#489 measured ~230 orphans/hour into swap |
| F4 | a reap stops a daemon of another key | it kills a healthy daemon that other clones' watches depend on; they go silent until their next push |
| F9 | one clone's watch on one pr lives under two keys | both daemons alert it; every event arrives twice |
| F11 | a reap stops a process that is not a pr-watch daemon of the caller's key (a reused pid) | it kills a stranger program the human was at work in |
| F10 | the daemon runs from, or sits in, the spawner's worktree | a delete or upgrade of that worktree takes the daemon down under every other clone; their alerts stop with no word |

### .impossible by nature

| # | the cell | why impossible |
|---|---|---|
| I1 | race × restart from one clone at once | one clone runs one findsert at a time |
| I2 | D8 deleted / upgraded × D2 absent | with no daemon alive, there is no spawner home to lose |

### .forbidden by nurture (alert invariants)

| # | the attempt | why forbidden (the harm) |
|---|---|---|
| F5 | alert a clone of its own comment | an echo loops a clone that replies to every alert. "own" means this clone, not the bot login every clone shares — a login filter would silence a peer clone's review (A20) |
| F6 | drop an alert the clone could not take | the mechanic misses the event; the director is back in the relay |
| F7 | deliver one event twice to one clone | the mechanic acts twice (a double fix, a double reply) and learns to distrust alerts |
| F8 | type an alert into a modal or over a human's typed text | a modal answer is the human's call; `clone say --force` fuses the human's text into the turn |
