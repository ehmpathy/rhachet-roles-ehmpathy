# vision: a quota refusal names the director grant, never a human

## the outcome world

### before
a driver under a director spends its last commit use. `git.branch.rebase begin` says "ask your human
to grant more". the driver believes it, halts, and pages for a human. its director held the grant
all along. measured 2026-10-27: the svc-quotes #141 row sat ~25 minutes.

### after
in a tree a director governs, each quota refusal says "ask your director", and prints the one call
the director runs, with the real tree slug:

```
a director governs this tree; ask your director to grant more. the director runs:
  $ rhx git.crew.grant --tree svc-quotes.beav.fix-ask-for-details-survives-crash --quota <n> --push allow --why '<why>'
```

the driver relays the ask; it never runs the grant itself. the director runs it once, and the driver
resumes. in a tree with no director, every refusal reads exactly as today.

### the aha
the refusal names whoever can actually lift it. the driver never has to know the org chart; the
text knows it.

## scope

in a director-governed tree, three refusals name the director grant (F2, ruled):

| refusal | git.commit.set | git.branch.rebase begin | git.release --mode apply | git.commit.push |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "no commit uses left" | yes (`:789`) | yes (`:98`) | yes (`:294`) | — (spends no use) |
| "push not allowed" | yes (`:800`) | yes (`:87`) | — (no push check) | yes (`:360`, tree + json modes) |
| "no commit quota set" | yes (`:762`) | yes (`:72`) | yes (`:281`) | — (meets the push refusal) |

also in scope (F3, ruled): this repo's `howto.git-commit-set.[lesson].md` and its `.min`, which say
"human must grant quota".

out: a block above the tree (C8, forbidden: a per-tree grant cannot lift it) and a corrupt quota
file (C7: a malfunction; its fix is `uses del`, then a grant) keep their extant fix. a tree with no
director keeps today's text on every refusal (F4, ruled).

## mental model

- the driver's words: "i'm out of commits — who do i ask?"
- the analogy: a contractor out of budget asks the site foreman, not the client. the foreman holds
  a budget for the site.
- our terms: director, charter, `git.crew.grant`, quota, uses. the driver needs only "director" and
  the line to relay.

## evaluation

- goal fit: the refusal names the right granter, so the driver asks the right party first time.
- pros: one director check, one render helper for three refusals; the no-director path stays
  byte-identical.
- cons:
  - this repo reads a fact (a director governs the tree) another repo writes — a cross-repo
    contract (F1, risk high).
  - the printed command lives in another repo (`uladkasach/nheuron`); a flag rename there goes stale
    here with no test to catch it (F5, risk medium).
- pit of success:
  - a director marker that cannot be read names both granters, director and human, and says so (C5).
    a fault never hides the fix, and a driver is never sent back to a human alone.
  - a block above the tree still names the human (C8): a director grant cannot lift it.

assumptions:
- `git.crew.grant` takes `--tree <slug> --quota N --push allow|block --why <text>`. read from
  nheuron's decision logs, not source: no `git.crew.grant.sh` is on nheuron's origin/main (F5).
- the driver reaches its director through its extant channel: its halt, which the director reads.
  this change adds no channel.
- the driver acts on the text it reads; its boot brief says "human" too, so F3 amends it.
- exit codes stay as they are per surface (rebase exits 1, set and release exit 2). the rebase
  mismatch is a caught dream (`dreams/v2026_10_27.fix.rebase-constraint-exit-code.md`).

## what is awkward?

- detection rests on a file another repo writes, at a path this repo must hard-code. a clamp can pin
  the path here; it cannot stop nheuron from a move.
- the printed grant line is coupled to a verb whose source is not on nheuron's origin/main.
- four surfaces render the refusals in three shapes (tree helper, raw echo, indented echo, json on
  push). one helper serves the text; each surface keeps its own frame.
