### 🔴 two findings the vision did not hold

1. **`find_claude_dir` forks `dirname` once per parent directory** (`blocklist.sh:87`,
   `gerunds.sh:115`). from a deep worktree that is ~7 execs, in a loop, on **every** invocation.
   ⇒ `${dir%/*}` is a bash expansion at **zero** forks.
2. 🔴 **the blocklist sweeps stale nudges BEFORE it detects a term** (`:106-110` precedes `:116`).
   3 execs to prune a nudge file the clean path will never read.
   ⚠️ **gerunds already sequences this correctly** — it exits at `:103` ahead of its sweep. so the
   two hooks disagree on sequence today, and only one of them is wrong.

⇒ **the wish's count was low because it counted the loop and stopped.** the ambient cost — a
`dirname` loop, a stale sweep, a `date` — is over a third of the clean path.

## 🔴 .a "hang" that was never a hang — three wrong diagnoses, in order

a full-hook sweep was run to confirm no adjacent suite broke. it sat `inflight` past 19 minutes.
**three readings followed, and the first two were written down before they were tested.**

| # | the diagnosis | how it was refuted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **runqueue contention** — two jest runs were live at the time | it sat inflight the same way when run **alone** |
| 2 | 🔴 **`permissionrequest.compose` hangs** — 96 tests, two bash spawns each | a 10-file scope that **excludes** it sat inflight too |
| 3 | ✅ **nothing hangs. these suites are simply slow, and the batch exceeded the wait** | a 3-file scope finished green in **109s** — so ~36s/file, and a 13-file scope needs ~8 minutes |

⇒ **the answer was arithmetic, not a defect.** each suite spawns dozens of bash processes; a
per-file cost near 36s means a large scope legitimately runs for minutes, and each poll that reads
`inflight` looks identical to a hang.

### 🔴 what makes this worth the record

**diagnosis #1 flattered this wish's own premise.** the fork-storm wish argues that fork latency
under contention is the real cost — so "two jest runs contended" was not merely plausible, it was
the thesis. it went into this yield as a first-hand demonstration.

⚠️ **a diagnosis that confirms your premise is the one you are least likely to re-check.** it was
refuted by the cheapest possible experiment, which was not run until after it was written down.

**diagnosis #2 was worse in a quieter way**: it was refuted by evidence already in hand. the 10-file
scope that also sat inflight **excluded** the suite being blamed, and that scope had been run
before the blame was recorded.

⇒ every correction is kept in place rather than swapped silently, per
`rule.forbid.chronological-accretion`'s exemption for a record whose purpose IS the sequence: the
sequence is the lesson.

### 🔴 the lessons that generalize past this wish

1. **an instrument that lies is the dominant failure class here** — seven instances, every one found
   by a reviewer or by a deliberate control, **none by a test that passed**. a clamp that cannot go
   red reports safety it never measured.
2. **a claim in prose drifts exactly as a test does, with no assertion to catch it.** the comment
   that named this change's blast radius overclaimed by one hook, in the direction that made
   coverage look wider.
3. **a correctly-deferred defect still owes a test of what is deferred**, or the deferral is
   indistinguishable from a miss.
4. **an extraction is bounded by its contract, never its intent** — one built to remove duplication
   left a third copy it could not serve.
5. **a deferral's escape hatch deserves the scrutiny its main argument gets.** the clause nobody
   weighed is the one that was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that made the deferral look
   free.
6. **a rule stated at the site of its own violation does not enforce itself** — four instances, one
   of them where the author and the reader of the rule were the same party.
7. 🔴 **"re-derive, never carry forward" is an act each edit repeats, never a property a document
   has.** a stale number is visibly old; a fresh delta on a stale base looks derived and is not.
8. 🔴 **a controlled experiment beats a plausible number, and it is usually cheaper.** the review
   lanes permafailed at 2GB for two rounds. I diagnosed it twice from correlations — the yield's line
   count, then the `--conversation` corpus — and both reads were reasoned, cited, and **wrong**; the
   second cost a guard edit that was reverted the same day. what settled it in under ten minutes was
   one control: *the same glob, one variable changed.* ⇒ the cause is `.temp/genTempDir.symlink` →
   **10,172 dirs** outside the repo, walked because `enumFilesFromGlob` applies its gitignore filter
   to the **result** rather than the **traversal**. with `.temp/**` ignored: **4,975 files in 2.6s**;
   without it: timeout. ⚠️ **the tell was on the page both times** — F13 was itemized at 70% with *"I
   never measured the OOM's source"* written into it. **a flagged low confidence names the next
   action; it does not excuse the omission.**
9. 🔴 **a lever discarded by a read is discarded on that read's weakest sentence.** F13 ruled out
   *"narrow `--paths-with`"* — the very remedy `rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions` names
   first — on the grounds that *"a narrow filters after the step that kills it."* the premise was
   right (the process does die upstream of the scope-debug write) and the inference was backwards:
   `--paths-with` is the **walk's own input** (`stepReview.js:266-269` → `:280`), while

   whose subject is the wrong noun.** it reads exactly like a sound refutation, and what parts them
   is a re-read of the cited lines rather than of the argument.

   a real failure, and only its clock says which world it measured. once the debts were paid, i017

10. 🔴 **a defect can live in a document's SUM while no single edit of it is wrong.** every round's
