# case=7 — the first day of a cloud tree

> cells **44 → 10 → 6 → 4 → 46 → 28** · two actors · one session
> feel **sharp** · care **critipath** · 🔴 **the densest walk in the box — 6 boundaries.**

⚠️ **feel is per-leg on a journey, and the journey takes the strictest.** its legs run
`sharp, sharp, sharp, happy, happy, sharp` — so the **sharp** obligation binds the whole walk, and
it must fail safe at every leg. ⇒ `sharp` is the honest single value here; a third value on a
closed axis (`define.experience._.axis=feel.path=happy-vs-sharp` admits exactly two) would be a
term invented to dodge a grade.

🔴 **the `[tn]` renders below are the SHIPPED renders**, copied from
`git.commit.sponsor.integration.test.ts.snap` and `git.commit.set.integration.test.ts.snap`. they
were sketches at vision time; verification re-rendered them so the contract and the code teach one
mental model (`rule.forbid.snapshot-visual-blemishes`).

⚠️ where a render repeats one already shown, the body is elided with `...` and a pointer — never
truncated into a **shorter** render, which is how this journey's `[t5]` once taught a one-line
refusal the skill has never emitted.

## .why this case exists

cases 1–6 are **spotlights**: each holds one boundary still and proves it fails safe. that is what
a sharp critipath owes (`define.experience._.axis=feel.path=happy-vs-sharp`).

but a spotlight cannot show a **boundary interaction**, and interactions are where defects cluster
(`define.experience._.metric=boundary-density`: *"each boundary is a failure point, and their
interactions compound"*).

⇒ this case walks one realistic session across **six** boundaries and two actors. it exercises
every cell cases 1–5 hold still, plus one they never reach (**cell 28**, the clear seam), and it
surfaces **four demands no single-cell demo can state**, plus one **felt** risk no `[tn]` can see.

⇒ the guardrail holds: this journey gives breadth; cases 3, 4, 5 keep their focused fail-safe
spotlights. one does not replace the other.

## .the narrative

ulad dispatches a cloud tree for a fix in `svc-jobs` and grants the quota. **the grant nudges him**
— `case=1` `[t0]`'s coconut fires, because no sponsor is bound to this tree:

```
🥥 did you know?
   ├─ no sponsor is bound to this tree, so commits will refuse
   ├─ printf 'Name <email>' | rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @stdin
   ├─ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who "Name <email>"
   └─ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @me   # reserve for YOUR OWN work
```

he skims it and moves on. the grant exits **0**, so the tree is provisioned and the mechanic starts
work with no sponsor bound.

⚠️ **the nudge fired and was ignored, and this case depends on that possibility.** an earlier draft
opened with *"he forgets the sponsor — his laptop never asked for it"*, which contradicted
`case=1`'s own guarantee that the grant **does** ask. 🔴 **caught at peer review r3**: the densest
walk in the contract began in a world the design claims to prevent.

⇒ and the premise survives the correction, which is the part worth a line: `case=1` says outright
that *"a human may ignore it, and then `case=3`'s refusal is the backstop."* **a coconut is optional
by construction** (`rule.require.coconut-hints` — a coconut that carries mandatory load grades a
blocker), so an ignored nudge is no defect in the nudge. ⇒ 🔴 **this journey is precisely the walk
that proves the backstop is owed.** a design with only the nudge has no answer here, and `F8`'s
choice — nudge **plus** refusal, never nudge alone — is what this case validates.

the mechanic works, finishes, and reaches for a commit. it is refused. it reads the refusal, sees a
command, and — a competent agent that runs commands — runs it. refused again, this time for who it
is rather than what it offered.

ulad ssh's in. he reaches for the form his laptop taught him, `--who @me`, and the tree tells him
what he did not know: on this host, the github session is the clone. he pipes his identity in
instead. the mechanic retries and the commit lands, named.

later he hands the tree to another supervisor and clears the sponsor. the very next commit refuses
again — the tree returns to the state it started in, and the loop closes.

⇒ **six boundaries, one afternoon, and the human learned the model at the moment they needed it.**

### 🔴 the felt risk the `[tn]` cannot show — three refusals before one yes

read the walk as ulad lives it rather than as a step list: **the tree says no three times before
it says yes.** the mechanic is refused, its own attempt to fix that is refused, and then *his*
first command is refused too.

each refusal is individually correct — the `[tn]` proves that, one at a time. but a `[tn]` grades
steps in isolation and **cannot see the accumulation**. from his seat the question is not *"was
that rejection valid?"* but *"is this feature broken?"* — and a human who concludes the latter
disables the guard rather than learns it.

⇒ **so the three refusals must read as ONE rule stated three times, never as three unrelated
rejections.** the shared line is *a sponsor is bound by a human, and it must be a human*:

| leg | the refusal says | the shared rule |
|---|---|---|
| t0 | no sponsor bound · **a human** binds it | who may bind |
| t1 | only **a human** can bind a sponsor | who may bind |
| t2 | that identity is a robot — **not a human** | what may be bound |

⇒ by t2 the third refusal should **confirm** the model the first two taught, not introduce a fourth
surprise. that is a demand on the refusals' *shared vocabulary*, and it is a demand only the
narrative form produces — the `[tn]` sees three correct rejections and no problem at all.

⚠️ this is the `do not transliterate` earn (`define.experience`): the `[tn]` catches the logical
holes (the four demands below), the narrative catches this **felt** one. neither form finds the
other's.

## .the bdd sketch

```
given [case7] a fresh cloud grove, tree dispatched
  and a commit quota of 5, push allowed
  and NO sponsor bound
  and `git config user.name` = `seaturtle[bot]`
  and changes are staged
```

**[t0] the mechanic commits — the refusal** · cell 44 · 🔴 boundary 1: rejection

```sh
$ printf 'fix(queue): drop the stale index\n\n- detail' | rhx git.commit.set -m @stdin --mode apply
🐢 bummer dude...

🐚 git.commit.set
   └─ error: no sponsor is bound to this tree

   a commit must name the human who authorized it. this tree names
   none, so there is nobody to answer for the change.

   ⛔ you cannot fix this yourself. a sponsor is bound by a human,
      at a tty.

ask your human to run, in this tree:
  $ printf 'Name <email>' | rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @stdin
  $ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who "Name <email>"
```

then: exit **2**, no commit made — the full fail-safe verdict is `case=3`
then: ✅ the printed forms are the **supplied** ones, which hold on **every** grove — `case=3`'s
rule. ⚠️ had it printed `@me`, `[t2]` would show the human who runs the refusal's own advice and is
refused again, which is the loop this journey exists to prove does not happen
then: 🔴 **and `[t2]` is the proof that the omission carries load rather than style.** ulad reaches
for `@me` from his own habit and is refused — so the design already knows what that form does here,
and printed the two that avoid it
then: 🔴 the refusal is now **input to the next step**, which is what a spotlight cannot show

**[t1] the mechanic runs the fix on itself** · cell 10 · 🔴 boundary 2: actor guard

```sh
$ printf 'Ada Lovelace <ada@example.com>' | rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @stdin
🐢 bummer dude...

🐚 git.commit.sponsor set
   └─ error: only humans can run this command

   a sponsor answers for a change in the real world, so only a
   human can bind or clear one.

   this holds even for a real human's name. to name a human who
   did not act is a fabrication, and it reads as authorized.

ask your human to run, in this tree:
  $ printf 'Name <email>' | rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @stdin
  $ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who "Name <email>"
```

then: exit **2** — the full verdict is `case=4`
then: 🔴 **the second paragraph is what shuts the retry loop.** the reader is a clone that just had
a value rejected, so the cheap lesson is *"pick a better name"* — which is the fabrication case.
the refusal names it outright rather than leave it to be inferred
then: 🔴 **it substituted a REAL human** — it read the name off the branch's git log, so the value
is truthful and the act is still forbidden. that is cell 10, and it is why the actor guard cannot
be replaced by a value check
then: ⚠️ this `[tn]` ran the `@me` form until the second council. the substitution keeps the walk
faithful to `[t0]`: the mechanic runs **the command the refusal printed**, and `[t0]` now prints
`--who @stdin`. ⇒ a journey whose `[t1]` ran a command `[t0]` never named would be a walk of the
design rather than of the human's actual path
then: 🔴 **DEMAND 1 — the refused bind writes no state.** the guard sits on the *actor*, so it
fires before the value is read and before any write. a guard that refused **after** a write would
leave a robot on record, and `[t3]` would then be an overwrite rather than a first bind
then: ⇒ this is checkable: after `[t1]`, `sponsor get` must still read `sponsor: (none)`
then: ⚠️ no spotlight states this. `case=4` proves the refusal; only the *sequence* proves the
refusal left the tree clean

**[t2] ulad ssh's in and reaches for his laptop habit** · cell 6 · 🔴 boundary 3: identity guard

```sh
$ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @me
🐢 bummer dude...

🐚 git.commit.sponsor set
   └─ error: that identity cannot answer for a change

   the github session on this host reads:
     Seaturtle of'Ehmpathy <259600029+ehm-seaturtle@users.noreply.github.com>
   ...and it is a robot.

   a sponsor answers for the change in the real world. a robot cannot
   answer, so it cannot hold the slot.

   🌊 on a cloud grove that session is the clone's, never yours.

name the human instead:
  $ printf 'Name <email>' | rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who @stdin
  $ rhx git.commit.sponsor set --who "Name <email>"
```

then: exit **2** — the full verdict is `case=5`
then: 🔴 **DEMAND 2 — the refusal carries the recovery, so `[t3]` needs no doc read.** the human
arrived with a wrong mental model and leaves with a right one plus the exact next command. a
refusal that merely said *"invalid identity"* would send them to `--help`, and the walk would stall
here rather than proceed

### 🔴 the second council changed this `[tn]` twice, and the second change is the interesting one

| what changed | why |
|---|---|
| the command ran `--from git-config` → **the `@me` form** | `F9` struck the seed; the trap moved to the paved form |
| the fix rendered as a **coconut** → **prose** | `Q2′` left no pick to opt into, so the coconut's warrant vanished |

⚠️ **an earlier draft of this file argued the opposite, and its argument was sound at the time:**
*"`[t0]`'s command is mandatory, so it renders as prose; `[t2]` offers a genuine pick between two
routes, so it renders as a coconut."* ⇒ the two routes were the `@me` form and a supplied one, and
`Q2′` refused the first **here**.

⚠️ **and the surface changed a third time, at `F10`** — the two flags became one, so the sentence
above now reads about **value forms** rather than flags. 🎯 **note what did NOT change: the
render.** the coconut's warrant is the *presence of a pick*, and `F10` altered the spelling of the
forms rather than whether the human has one. ⇒ a render rule anchored to a flag name would have
needed a fourth revision; anchored to the pick, it needed none.

⇒ 🎯 **so the coconut lost its warrant, not its style.** `rule.require.coconut-hints` grades a
coconut that carries mandatory load a **blocker**, and with the `@me` route refused the load became
mandatory. ⇒ the render follows the **choice**, and the choice is a property of the design rather
than of the page.

⚠️ **and the two lines `[t2]` now prints are NOT a pick.** they are one mandatory act — *supply the
human* — in two spellings, and a human who takes neither is stuck. ⇒ **the test is whether the human
may decline, never how many lines are printed** (`case=5` `[t0]` states the same call).

⚠️ **and the case loses real ground by it.** this journey used to show both render shapes in one
session, which is where an inconsistency is most visible to a reviewer. it now shows one.

🔴 **and the pair it was said to move to does not exist.** the replacement was `case=3` `[t1]`'s
coconut beside `case=3` `[t0]`'s prose — but that coconut never shipped and should not: `case=3`'s
refusal is grove-invariant, so it cannot carry a grove-specific `@me` shorthand, and its reader is
a clone that may act on no hint at all. ⇒ **the both-shapes-in-one-session demonstration is simply
absent from the catalog**, on record rather than papered over. the pair that remains is a coconut
at `case=1` `[t0]` beside prose at `case=3` `[t0]` — two files, two sessions.
⇒ `1.vision.yield.md` → Q5.

**[t3] ulad binds the human, piped** · cell 4 · boundary 4: the happy bind

then: the full verdict is `case=1`
then: 🔴 `source: supplied`, never `me` — ulad handed a value in rather than read this host's own

then: 🔴 this is a **first** bind, not an overwrite — because of DEMAND 1
then: the two prior refusals cost the human one command each and no cleanup
then: ⚠️ **ulad names himself here, and that is still correct** — `Q8` ruled the sponsor is the

**[t5] ulad clears the sponsor to hand the tree over** · cell 28 → cell 44 · 🔴 boundary 6: the seam

```sh
$ rhx git.commit.sponsor del
🐢 groovy, sponsor cleared

🐚 git.commit.sponsor del
   └─ cleared
```

```sh
$ printf 'fix(queue): one more\n\n- detail' | rhx git.commit.set -m @stdin --mode apply
🐢 bummer dude...

🐚 git.commit.set
   └─ error: no sponsor is bound to this tree
   ... (the same body and remedy as [t0] — the tree is back where it started)
```

then: 🔴 **DEMAND 3 — the clear takes effect on the VERY NEXT commit.** the sponsor is read per
commit, never cached per process or per session. a design that read it once at startup would let a
cleared tree keep the commit — a stale authorization, which is the defect's own shape
then: ⇒ the tree returns to `[t0]`'s state exactly. the loop closes, and the state has one source
then: ⚠️ **cell 28 is `itemized` in the inventory** (`human × clear × human × cloud`, alter — a
rebind is cheap). its *care* verdict stands: as a lone act it costs ≈ 0. what earns it a walk here

## 🔴 .the fourth demand — a refused commit must not spend quota

the `given` grants a quota of **5** and `[t0]` is refused. that forces a question no single-cell
demo asks: **did the refusal cost a use?**

if it did, a tree with quota 5 burns its grant on five refusals and then needs **two** human grants
to recover — the quota *and* the sponsor. the sponsor guard would have manufactured a second
blocker out of the first.

✅ **the extant structure already answers it correctly, and the repair must not disturb the order:**

⚠️ **the order is the claim, so the order is what this table cites** — not line numbers. an earlier
draft pinned four absolute lines in `git.commit.set.sh`; **the sponsor guard this very wish adds is
what shifted every one of them.** ⇒ a doc that cites a line number into a file the same change
edits is stale before it is read.

| # | step in `git.commit.set.sh` | grep for | act |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | quota **read** + check | `no commit uses left` | read-only |
| 2 | 🔴 the sponsor **read** + guard | `no sponsor is bound to this tree` | read-only |
| 3 | stage, commit, then push | `git add -A`, `PUSH_RESULT_STATUS=` | the mutations |
| 4 | quota **write** (the decrement) | `meter` | 🔴 **last act in the file** |

⇒ a sponsor refusal exits **hundreds of lines before** the meter is written — and a refusal sited
beside the other guards would too. **the property holds under either site, because the meter write
is the last act of the file.**

⚠️ **so this demand is a preservation, and the risk is low.** it earned a line anyway because the
property was **invisible in the code** — no comment marked that write as order-sensitive — and the
question only arose because the walk grants a quota and then gets refused. a spotlight that starts
from *"a sponsor is bound"* never meets it.

✅ **and it is invisible no longer.** the shipped guard carries the reason in its own header:
*"it sits ahead of the meter write (the last act of this skill), so a refused commit spends no
quota. otherwise a tree would burn its grant on refusals and need TWO human grants to recover."*
⇒ what the walk unearthed became a durable comment at the site it governs, which is where an
invariant survives a refactor.

⇒ invariant for `2.1.criteria`: **the sponsor guard runs before the meter write.**

## 🔴 .the four demands, and why only a journey produces them

each is a checkable property of a **transition**, not of a state. no spotlight can state one,
because a spotlight holds the rest of the world still.

| # | the demand | the transition | what breaks without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | a refused bind writes no state | t1 → t3 | a robot on record; the human's bind becomes an overwrite, and a failed overwrite leaves the robot |
| **2** | the refusal carries the recovery | t2 → t3 | the walk stalls at a `--help` read; the paved path is paved only in the happy case |
| **3** | the sponsor is read per commit | t5 → next | a cleared tree keeps its commit — a stale authorization, the defect's own shape |
| **4** | a refused commit spends no quota | t0 → t4 | five refusals burn the grant; recovery then needs two human grants, not one |

⇒ **demand 3 is the one this walk exists for.** `case=2` proves a bound sponsor yields a named
commit; it cannot prove that an *unbound* sponsor stops one, because that needs two commits with a
mutation between them.

## .what makes it a critipath

| term | value |
|---|---|
| frequency | 🔴 **every fresh cloud tree, until the step becomes habit** — this is the onboard walk |
| cost if it breaks mid-walk | the human is stranded on a tree that cannot commit and cannot tell them why |
| workable fallback | **none** — each boundary's fallback is the next boundary in this same walk |

⇒ **critipath.** and it is the one a human actually *lives*; the others are the frames it passes
through.
