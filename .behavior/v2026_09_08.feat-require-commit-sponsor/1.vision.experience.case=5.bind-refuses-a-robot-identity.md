# case=5 — the bind refuses a robot identity

## 🔴 ✅ RULED — the trap moved from the seed to `--who @me`

this case was written around the **seed**: a human on a cloud grove reaches for
`--from git-config`, and `git config` there is the clone.

**the wisher struck the seed at the second council** (`F9`), so that route no longer exists. ⇒ the
trap did not go away — **it moved to the other convenient supply**, and the guard is unchanged.

| the human reaches for | reads | on a cloud grove it holds |
|---|---|---|
| ~~`--from git-config`~~ | the tree's git config | the clone — ⛔ **route struck** |
| 🔴 **`--who @me`** | `gh api user` under `as-human` | the clone (`Q2′`, measured) |

⇒ 🎯 **the case is stronger for the move, not weaker.** the seed was a route the design *chose* to
offer and could simply retract; `@me` is the **paved local form**, so its cloud misfire cannot be
designed away — only guarded. **the guard now carries load rather than sits optional.**

⚠️ **and the trap moved a THIRD time, at `F10`.** the drive then carried `--from me` as a flag of
its own; the wisher asked why it was not a value of `--who`, and it folded. ⇒ the hazard survived
two removals unchanged, which is the tell that it is intrinsic: **`@me` means "the identity this
host says is me", and a cloud grove genuinely answers that with the clone.**

## .the narrative

a human is on a cloud grove, over ssh. they reach for the convenient form — `--who @me` — because
that is what they do on their laptop and it works there.

on this host the github session **is the clone's**. the convenient form offers a robot.

⇒ 🔴 **one value form, two meanings, decided by an axis the human cannot see from the prompt.** the human
is genuinely present, genuinely at a tty, and genuinely doing the right thing as they understand it.
nothing about the *act* is wrong. only the **value** is.

⇒ ⚠️ **and that is precisely why the act check cannot catch this one.** `F3` ruled the primary guard
is an ACT check — a tty plus a permission the clone lacks. here a real human holds both. **the act
check passes, and the identity backstop is what refuses.**

⇒ 🎯 **so this case is the demonstration that the backstop is OWED.** `F3` demoted the identity
check to a secondary net; `case=5` `[t0]` is the cell where the primary net has nothing to catch.

⚠️ **the vision fixes the SHAPE and not the exact words, and the split is deliberate.** the stem's

## .the shape of the guard — establish a human, do not enumerate robots

the wish is explicit: *"the guard must establish a **human**, never enumerate known-bad names."*
the extant placeholder guard is the counter-example — two literals where it means a class.

🔴 **and a naive robot pattern over-rejects — measured, not inferred.** `#645` proposes
`*users.noreply.github.com`. on `ahbode/svc-jobs` main, that domain carries **both** a bot
(`rhelease[bot]`) and a **human** (`Uladzimir Kasacheuski <uladkasach@users.noreply.github.com>`).

⇒ the pattern would reject the wisher himself. an email domain cannot establish a human.
full evidence: `F3`, and `1.vision.yield.md` → external research.

⇒ this vision does **not** settle the guard's exact predicate. what it settles is the **bar**:

| the guard must | why |
|---|---|
| reject a value that cannot answer for a change | the motive the term is built on |
| run at **bind**, where a human can fix it | not at commit, where only a clone reads it |
| ⛔ not reject a human's private-email address | over-rejection makes the paved path unusable |
| ⛔ not be a list of known-bad names | a list cannot establish a class; the next bot is not on it |

⇒ the predicate is a fulcrum — `F3` — and it wants a domain-discovery pass of its own, likely at
`2.1.criteria`.
