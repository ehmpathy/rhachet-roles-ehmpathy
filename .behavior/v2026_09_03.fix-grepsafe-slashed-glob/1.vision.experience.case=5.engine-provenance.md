# case=5 · the answer names the engine that produced it

> rides every pattern-hits cell · **happy** · **critipath** · acceptance 4

## .the narrative

a clone gets a zero from a slashed glob:

```
🐚 grepsafe
   ├─ pattern: the bar was MET
   ├─ glob: .agent/**/*.md
   ├─ engine: rg
   └─ matches: 0
```

one line, and the zero is attributable. without it, a caller who knows two engines mean different
things by `--glob` still cannot act on it.

## .the five divergences — why two engines were never equal

F8 + F9 ("zero synonymous codepaths") deleted the `grep` arm: one engine, `rg`, and a failfast where
it is absent (case=11). these divergences cannot arise in the delivered code. the table stays as the
diagnosis that justified the deletion.

| # | divergence | `rg` | `grep` | outcome |
|---|-----------|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | glob semantics | matches the path | matches the basename | case=1 |
| 2 | gitignore | skipped by default | reads no `.gitignore` | case=9 (F3) |
| 3 | hidden paths | skipped on traversal | not skipped | case=8 (F6) |
| 4 | regex dialect | Rust regex, `\d`, unicode | POSIX ERE, no `\d` | unclosable while two engines exist |
| 5 | a link named literally | not followed — a false zero | not measured | case=8 (F6) |

> bash **expands** a glob into path operands, so a literal name resolves through a link.
> `rg` **filters a walk**, so a path the walk never enters cannot be rescued by any glob text.

neither engine lets `**` cross a link, which is why a pnpm `node_modules` looks shallow to both. that
agreement is not the absence of a defect: `.agent/`'s briefs are links, so both were wrong for the
caller.

## .why a critipath

the divergence count grew three times under inspection. a surface that grows under inspection is the
surface that needs provenance in its output. the diagnostician's first question — "which engine?" —
is answered on screen.

a human re-asks after a zero; a clone concludes and writes the conclusion down. no help text reaches
the clone, so provenance rides in the answer, never in docs or a `--verbose` flag.
