IMPACTS OF RURAL ELECTRIFICATION IN RWANDA: A CASE STUDY OF EASTERN PROVINCE AND SOME SUBURBS
Unknown author


The review will assess the impact that schemes for rural electrification have had on small business development and income generating activities and on access and affordability in Rwanda. Affordability is of course, related to household income and opportunities to earn income, as well as income or concessions provided through various types of policy interventions (e.g. implicit and explicit in the design of tariff and subsidization policies). Affordability is also integrally affected by wider issues, such as participation in community-based initiatives and the availability of localized credit facilities to help develop and finance access and use of energy.
Pico-Micro hydro installations are typically used for provision of electricity to isolated communities in developing countries that are not connected to the national grid or whose expansion is slow. Though localized electrification program and use of Pico-micro hydro installations, local businesses and households can obtain access and connection to electricity and not only control this expansion, but also the unit cost of the power consumed.
Following the 1994 April Genocide in Rwanda, the Government found an almost non-existent energy sector and a failed attempt at liberalization by the previous regime. The current Government passed the Rwanda Energy Policy 2011, and in its implementation has highlighted and encouraged the role of the private business and communities as drivers to achieve the policy goals. The Energy Policy objective is to improve electricity connectivity and access through the localized electrification program. This paper critically analyses Rwanda’s Energy Policy, Millennium Vision 2020 goals, and the current electricity demand and supply, as well as the private-community power localized electricity program in achieving the said Energy Policy. This paper further analyses the impact and constraints of the pico-micro hydroelectricity program; the role of the private sector and community involvement in achieving the Energy Policy objectives. The findings indicate that although the impact of the localized electrification program is constrained by various factors, if ably addressed by the Government and development partners, it can have a more positive impact on the achievement of connectivity and access of the local population and attainment of the Millennium 2020 goals [2]
As mentioned before, the rural electrification process in Rwanda has been successful, especially when compared to other developing countries, not only in the East African region but Continent-wide. However, it is facing many challenges and, in order to achieve the standards of most of the developing areas, several issues need to be tackled.
Capacity and management experience: As mentioned above, the war and genocide wiped out a large portion of the human resource capacity in Rwanda, in both public and private sectors. While the returnees have brought in substantial knowledge of their own, they generally have had very limited experience in designing and executing government programs.
