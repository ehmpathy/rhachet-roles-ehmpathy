# 🌙 dream: a transient limit is a FIFTH malfunction cause

- **kind**: reseed
- **owner**: `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain` — `role=driver`
- **caught**: 2026-09-10

## .the cue that fired

two level-3 reviewers on stone `5.1.execution.from_vision` returned `unreadable — no numeric
count found`, exit 1. their stdout held one line:

```
You've hit your limit · resets 4am (UTC)
```

`rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions` owes a diagnosis for every malfunction, and it
names **four** candidate causes. i walked all four. **not one of them fits.**

## .the gap, precisely

the rule's table, verbatim in its four rows:

| candidate | what it prescribes |
|---|---|
| **driver-fixable** — a bad glob, an absent supply file, a malformed rubric path | fix it, answer its given, re-arrive |
| **a context overflow** | narrow the REVIEWER, in the guard |
| **human-fixable** — an absent credential, a permission gate, a foreman-only grant | surface the **exact command** |
| unclear | read stderr and logs, classify, then route |

a transient upstream limit is **none of these**, and the two nearest rows each point a driver
at a wrong act:

- ⛔ **driver-fixable** invites a bind edit on a lane whose bind was never exercised. the
  reviewer died before it read its arguments, so a narrowed `--paths-with` changes what the
  lane reads and leaves the cause untouched — and then reads as a repair
- ⛔ **human-fixable** invites an escalation that asks a human to grant what no human can
  grant. the message names no credential and no permission. **it names a clock**

⇒ the honest remedy is **time**: a re-run, later. that is the one act the table has no row for.

## .the discriminator — it shares a SYMPTOM with the overflow row

this is why the gap is dangerous rather than merely absent. a driver who reaches for the
nearest row will reach for **overflow**, because the two look identical at the verdict:

| | heap exhaustion | 🔴 a transient limit |
|---|---|---|
| verdict | `unreadable` | `unreadable` |
| exit code | 1 | 1 |
| log dir | empty | empty |
| **stderr** | `FATAL ERROR: Reached heap limit … 1.9GB` | `You've hit your limit · resets 4am (UTC)` |
| **time to death** | ~220s, in file-gather | ~3s, before any work at all |
| remedy | 🔴 a **guard edit** | ⏳ a **re-run** |

⇒ **the two discriminators are the stderr text and the time to death**, and neither is in the
verdict a driver reads first. measured here on one stone, one day: nine l1 lanes died of the
first cause and two l3 lanes died of the second, with the same headline.

## .the shape of the fix

in `.agent/repo=bhrain/role=driver/briefs/rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions.md`:

1. **add the fifth row** to the cause table:

   | 🔴 **transient** — an upstream limit with a stated reset | ⏳ **time.** re-arrive after the stated window |

2. **add the discriminator note** beside it — the stderr text and the time to death are what
   separate this from the overflow row, since the verdict does not

3. **name the anti-act**, in the same voice the rule already uses for the hand-run review:
   ⛔ *do NOT narrow a bind against a transient limit. the bind is innocent; the reviewer never
   reached its arguments*

4. **add the escalation bound** — a limit that does **not** clear past its own stated window is
   no longer transient. the honest next step is a halt with the message verbatim, rather than a
   third re-arrival on a hope (`rule.always.raise-a-blocker-a-taken-cannot-close`:
   *budget left is not evidence of a remedy*)

## .why it is not done in this round

the SAFE/CLEAN test (`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`):

- **safe?** yes — a doc edit to a brief
- **clean?** 🔴 **no.** the brief lives in `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhrain`, and this tree is
  `ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-ehmpathy`. the fix is not in the diff already open, and a tree adopts
  only what is scoped to itself

⇒ a **reseed**, per the kind table. the fix belongs to the repo that owns the rule.

## ⚠️ .one claim that must NOT enter the brief

my `.taken` for these two lanes reasons that *the level-3 lanes met the cap first because they
enroll an implementation clone — the most expensive call on the ladder.*

that is **n=1**. two lanes, one stone, one day. it is a plausible account of why these two and
not the other nine, and it is not evidence. ⇒ **do not write it into the rule.** if a second
stone shows the same order — expensive lanes first — it earns a line then.

what IS measured, and safe to write: the stderr text differs, and the time to death differs by
two orders of magnitude.
