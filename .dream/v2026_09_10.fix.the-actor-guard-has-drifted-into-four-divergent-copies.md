**behavior preserved exactly** — 336 tests green, no snapshot moved. all three call sites are

⇒ **what the extraction discharged: the UNDOCUMENTED half.** the function's name states its
mechanism — `…_via_stdin` — and its header states the asymmetry outright. the defect was never that
three copies existed; it was that a reader of any one had no way to learn the other three answer

⇒ **what it did NOT discharge, and must not**: the trio still checks stdin alone and still emits
stdout-only. ⚠️ the dedupe **preserves** the weak contract on purpose — to tighten it is a call
about who may grant commit authority.

🎯 **and the extraction makes the council's verdict CHEAPER: a tighten is now one line, not three.**

## .the gap, precisely

one question — *"is a human at this terminal?"* — is answered **twice**, two different ways
(it was four times until the dedupe above):

| site | streams checked | emits to |
|---|---|---|
| `git.commit.sponsor.sh` | `-t 2 && -t 1 && -t 0` | stdout **and** stderr |
| `guard_actor_is_human_via_stdin` — shared by all 3 `uses` skills | stdin only | stdout only |

🔴 **the three weaker copies are the ones that guard a QUOTA GRANT.** a caller with stdin attached
and stdout piped passes their check and fails the sponsor's — so the surface that hands out commit
authority is guarded more loosely than the one that names a human, and no comment anywhere says so.

🟡 **and the stdout-only emit breaks `rule.require.skill-output-streams`** outright: a failure must
ride both streams, so a refused grant is invisible to any log aggregator that reads stderr.

⇒ the divergence is **undocumented**, which is the part that makes it a defect rather than a
choice. a reader of any one copy has no way to learn that three others answer differently.

## .the shape of the fix

✅ **step 1 is DONE** — the shared home exists and the trio calls it. what is left is the tighten:

1. ~~extract the trio into `git.commit.operations.sh`~~ ✅ **landed 2026-09-11**, behavior-preserved
2. widen `guard_actor_is_human_via_stdin` to the **stronger** contract — all three streams, both
   output streams, the `__I_AM_HUMAN` escape hatch preserved — and **rename it** to
   `guard_actor_is_human`, since the `…_via_stdin` suffix names the weakness it would remove
3. fold `git.commit.sponsor.sh`'s own copy into it, so the count goes 2 → 1
4. one test per caller that the refusal now rides stderr too

⚠️ **step 2 is the behavior change, and it is the whole council question.** steps 3 and 4 are
mechanical once it is ruled.

## .why it is not done in this round

the SAFE/CLEAN test (`rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`) — **CLEAN refuses it**:

- it **changes the behavior of three extant skills** this change never intended to open. a caller
  that passes their guard today (stdin attached, stdout piped) would begin to be refused
- ⇒ that is a real tightening, and it is the right one — but it is a **behavior change to the quota
  surface**, which is a decision of its own rather than a tidy-up beside a sponsor guard
- their snapshots move too, so the diff reaches well past `git.commit.sponsor`

⚠️ **what is NOT a reason: "the three copies came first."** they did, and the sponsor's stronger
copy is what makes their weakness legible — this change is what surfaced the gap, so it owes the
record even where it does not owe the fix.

## .the bound worth a record

the extraction is the **easy** half. the hard half is the decision the extraction forces: **do the
three `uses` guards tighten to the sponsor's contract, or does the sponsor loosen to theirs?**

⇒ the answer is surely "tighten" — a quota grant is authority, and it should be guarded at least as
hard as a name on a commit. but that is a call about who may grant commit authority, and it wants a
wisher rather than a refactor.
