## .the gap, precisely

`.agent/repo=bhrain/role=driver/skills/route.mutate.guard.sh` declares the opposite of a mutation

```
# .what = pretooluse hook to protect route stones, guards, and metadata
# .why  = prevents drivers from:
#         - reading guard files (enables gaming review criteria)
# privilege:
#   human can grant via: rhx route.mutate grant allow
#   creates flag at: $route/.route/.privilege.mutate.flag
```

it is the **guard that refuses a driver's edit of `.guard` files**, and it names the grant as
**human**-owned. confirmed live on this route:

```
$ rhx route.mutate --grant get
🗿 route.mutate grant get
   ├─ route = .behavior/v2026_09_09.fix-hook-fork-storm

```

⚠️ **both invocation forms work** — `grant get` and `--grant get` alike. I briefly claimed the
positional form had regressed, on the strength of **one** errored invocation; four later calls
falsified it. that claim is struck, and its dream retired:
`.dream/v2026_09_17.fix.route-mutate-positional-subcommand-lookup-misses.md`.

## ✅ .the verdict — CONFIRMED by the human, 2026-09-18

the wisher ran `rhx route.mutate grant allow` themselves. ⇒ **the grant is human-owned in practice,
exactly as this dream claims, and the owner table's `driver` row is wrong.** the dream needed no
argument in the end; the halt it produced was the proof.

⚠️ **and the cost it predicted was paid in full**: the halt ran three days, cost a human round-trip,
and the driver spent that time on a diagnosis that the owner table's wrong row had made necessary.

⇒ **the one remedy both malfunction briefs prescribe for an overflowed lane sits behind a human
grant, while the owner table files it under `driver`.**

⚠️ **and this is the hazard `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation` warns about, inverted.**
the brief warns that *an absent row reads as "not mine."* here a **present** row reads as "mine" and
is not — which is the worse direction, because an absent row costs a driver a thought and a wrong
row costs a driver a full diagnosis pass before the truth surfaces.

🔴 **the second-order cost is the one that matters**: `rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews` rests its whole
argument on this row. it tells a driver *"every real need behind a hand-run review is served by a
guard edit"* and *"the recipe existed because the guard-edit lever was absent from the owner
table."* if the lever is human-gated, a driver who obeys the forbid rule is left with **no** driver
lever for an overflowed lane at all.

## .the shape of the fix

pick one of two; they are not equivalent.

| # | shape | what it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **fix the doc** — move the `route.mutate.guard` row to the **human** column in `rule.always.spend-own-levers-before-escalation`, and amend `rule.always.diagnose-reviewer-malfunctions` + `rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews` so the overflow remedy reads *"ask a human for `rhx route.mutate grant allow`, then narrow the bind"* | the briefs match the code. cheap, and it concedes that an overflowed lane is a human-gated halt |
| **B** | **fix the code** — let a driver narrow a bind's `--paths-with` / `--conversation` **without** a grant, while a read of the rubric body stays refused | the briefs become true as written. the guard exists to keep a driver away from the rubric's criteria; a scope narrow discloses none of them, so the two privileges may be separable |

⇒ **B is the shape the briefs already assume**, and it is the one that keeps
`rule.forbid.hand-run-reviews` honest. A is the fallback if the split turns out unsound — for
instance if a `--paths-with` narrow can itself skew a rubric by starvation of its evidence.

⚠️ **whichever lands, all three briefs move together.** the row, the malfunction remedy, and the
forbid rule's justification are one claim stated in three places, and a fix to one alone leaves the
other two false.
